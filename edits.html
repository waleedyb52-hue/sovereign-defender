Sovereign Defender report: proposed edits (second edition)

Targeted edits only. The structure, tables, figures and numbers stay as they are. Each item gives where it goes and the replacement text.

1. Abstract: put both figures side by side

Where: replace the abstract from "A subsequent 48-hour engineering pass…" to the end.

New text:

A subsequent 48-hour engineering pass added structural SQL-injection analysis, command-injection and path-traversal patterns, and linear-time rewrites of all super-linear regular expressions. We evaluate the detector before and after this pass on identical data, using a six-rung test-set ladder. The gain is real but narrow. On a sealed half of HttpParamsDataset, generated by the same tools as the development data, recall rose from 40.54% to 97.28% (95% CI 96.83–97.66%) with no false positive among 9,664 benign values. On a second, independently authored corpus (Fwaf), recall rose only from 38.94% to 41.60% (95% CI 40.24–42.97%). All detection results reproduced exactly on a second host. The improvement therefore reflects coverage of one generator's payload templates rather than robustness to attacks from other sources, and we argue that the two figures must always be reported together.

Why: the current abstract gives 97.28% a sentence of its own and attaches 41.60% with "only". The new wording gives them equal weight and states the conclusion before the reader reaches page 6.

2. New: executive summary (box after the abstract)

Where: a framed box between the abstract and Section I.

Text:

Executive summary

Improved: SQL-injection recall on sqlmap-style traffic rose from 39% to 99.9%, with zero false alarms. A padding bypass and 12 regular-expression denial-of-service vectors were closed. Same-day throughput on the project laptop rose by 84%.

Not improved: recall on attacks from another source moved only from 38.9% to 41.6%. Cross-site scripting stayed at 83%, payload-free scanner probes and remote file inclusion stay near 0%, and the kernel drop path remains simulated.

Decisions needed: (1) choose the operating point, τ = 25 (97.3%) or τ = 55 (95.9%); (2) keep the detector as one layer among several, not the sole control; (3) approve the next priority, a third independent dataset scored once before any further SQL-injection claim.

Why: company readers (management, clients) need the result and the decision in half a page.

3. Make clear who did the evaluation

Where: start of Section IV (Methodology), before "All detection results in this edition…".

New text (fill in the brackets):

This evaluation was carried out by [name / role], who [did / did not] write the detection rules changed by the pass. In this report, "the project" refers to the development team and the result files it committed; "this work" refers to measurements re-run independently for this edition.

Why: the report uses "we" while also referring to "the project" as a third party. Without this sentence a reader may take the evaluation for an independent audit.

Reference points: Work on intrusion detection under concept drift shows that detectors decay over time and that naive retraining forgets earlier attacks; the project therefore gates any retraining through a challenger model (planned Phase 3).

References: delete [3] and renumber.

5. The Fwaf false-positive count is an upper bound

Where: end of the second paragraph of Section V-D (after "…but for an unintended reason. The twelve flags on the sealed benign side were not inspected.").

Add:

Since ten of the eleven inspected flags on the readable benign file are mislabelled attacks, the Fwaf false-positive counts in Table IV are best read as upper bounds, and the projected precision of 87.62% at 2% prevalence as a lower bound.

And in Section VII, "Label quality", add:

Because the mislabelling runs in both directions, the Fwaf false-positive figures overstate the true false-alarm rate, and the recall figures may be understated or overstated.

7. Check before distributing outside the company (not a text change)

  • Fwaf licence: the repository states no licence. Using it internally is fine, but check with legal before the report is published or sent to clients.
  • Publishing 41.6%: stating the out-of-distribution figure openly builds credibility, but make sure the company has approved putting it in front of external readers.
